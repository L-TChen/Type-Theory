# Lecture 1: in-class unit plan and concrete slide changes

This plan adapts Lecture 1 to a three-hour unit with on-site exercises and no homework. The goal is that students leave the room able to parse, rename, substitute, and reduce small lambda terms.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the unit, students should be able to:

1. parse a lambda term into an abstract syntax tree;
2. identify binding structure, free variables, and bound variables;
3. explain alpha-equivalence by binding structure rather than variable names;
4. perform capture-avoiding substitution on small examples;
5. perform single-step and multi-step beta-reductions;
6. distinguish `=_alpha`, `->_beta`, `->>_beta`, and `=_beta`.

## Proposed three-hour schedule

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00--0:10 | Objectives and motivating examples from anonymous functions |
| 0:10--0:35 | Syntax, ASTs, and parsing conventions |

## Concrete slide edits

1. Replace the opening grading/homework frame with a `Unit goals` frame. The current frame assumes homework and an exam; it should not appear in a no-homework unit.
2. Fix the arithmetic-precedence example: `3 * 4 + 7 * 2` should mean `(3 * 4) + (7 * 2)`, not `(3 * 4) + (7 + 2)`.
3. Add a worked example immediately before the AST exercise:
   - parse `lambda x. x y`;
   - show why it means `lambda x. (x y)`;
   - draw the tree.
4. Turn the AST exercise into a two-stage activity:
   - minimum target: draw items 1--3;
   - challenge target: draw items 4--5 and explain convention use.
5. Before the formal alpha-equivalence rule, add a quick classification slide:
   - `lambda x. x` vs `lambda y. y`: yes;
   - `lambda x. y` vs `lambda y. y`: no;
   - `lambda x. lambda y. x` vs `lambda y. lambda x. y`: yes.
6. Mark de Bruijn representation as optional or intuition-only. It is useful, but not necessary for the same-day target of alpha-equivalence and substitution.
7. Add a worked capture example before the substitution definition:
   - naive substitution `[x/y](lambda x. y)` would incorrectly produce `lambda x. x`;
   - correct capture-avoiding substitution first renames the binder.
8. Replace the placeholder summary frame with a real table:

| Notation | Meaning | Directional? | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `t =_alpha u` | same binding structure | no | `lambda x. x =_alpha lambda y. y` |
| `t ->_beta u` | one beta step | yes | `(lambda x. x) z -> z` |
| `t ->>_beta u` | zero or more beta steps | yes | `t -> t` by zero steps |
| `t =_beta u` | equality generated by beta-reduction | no | if `t ->_beta u`, then `t =_beta u` |

9. Move Church booleans, Church numerals, and the Y combinator to an optional appendix or a separate advanced segment. They are good material but too much after binding/substitution/beta-reduction in a no-homework format.
10. Replace the final homework slide with an exit check:
    - compute `FV(lambda x. x z)`;
    - decide whether `lambda x y. x` and `lambda y x. y` are alpha-equivalent;
    - reduce `((lambda x. x) y) ((lambda z. z) x)` by two beta steps;
    - state one situation where substitution must rename a binder.

## In-class exercise set

### Exercise 1: parsing and ASTs

Draw ASTs for:

1. `x (y z)`;
2. `x y z`;
3. `lambda s z. s z`;
4. `(lambda x. x) (lambda y. y)`;
5. `lambda a b. a (lambda c. a b)`.

Suggested timing: 5 minutes individual, 5 minutes pair discussion, 5 minutes solution.

### Exercise 2: free variables

Compute free variables for:

1. `x y`;
2. `lambda x. x y`;
3. `lambda x. lambda y. x y z`;
4. `(lambda x. y) x`.

Suggested timing: 6 minutes individual, 4 minutes discussion.

### Exercise 3: beta-reduction

Circle all beta-redexes and reduce one step at a time:

1. `(lambda x. x) z`;
2. `((lambda x. x) y) ((lambda z. z) x)`;
3. `(lambda x y. y) (x y)`.

Suggested timing: 10 minutes plus solution.

## Material to keep as optional challenge

- de Bruijn representation;
- Church encodings;
- Y combinator and general recursion;
- Church--Rosser homework problem.

These are suitable as extra reading or follow-up material, but they should not be required for the three-hour in-class comprehension target.
